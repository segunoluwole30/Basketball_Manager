# Animated players: sources and licences

Everything the 3D view's animated players need, where it came from and what it may be
used for. In the web app a visitor fetches the loader modules (0.15 MB) and one model: `player.glb`
(1.26 MB) or `player_light.glb` (0.77 MB, the same player with about 40% of the triangles),
only when 3D is first shown. Everything below applies to both models.

| File | What | Source | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `assets/player.glb` (body: skin, face, hands, eyes) | A tall athletic male body, about 5,200 triangles after simplifying, skinned to 23 joints, with a second "heavy" shape as a morph target | MakeHuman (<http://www.makehumancommunity.org>): the base mesh, the default rig's skin weights, the shape targets (male, young, muscle, weight, height, proportions, and its three ethnic face targets: an even mix for the body, each one as a face morph target) and the eyeball helper mesh, fetched from <https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman> by `tools/build_player_assets.py` | CC0 1.0, see below |
| `assets/player.glb` (kit, hair and gear) | Jersey, shorts, socks, shoes; six hair parts, beard, headband, arm and leg sleeves, wristbands, brows. About 10,900 triangles in all, of which a player shows about 7,000 | Generated by `tools/build_player_assets.py` in this repository. The jersey, the shorts, socks, the hair cap, beard, sleeves and bands are layers lifted off the MakeHuman surface; the shoes and the larger hair styles are fitted to the body by measuring it | Same as the rest of this project (the lifted layers: CC0 geometry) |
| `assets/player.glb` (all 30 animation clips) | Motion capture, retargeted to the model's skeleton, cut, put in place and resampled to 30 Hz | Carnegie Mellon University Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database, <http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu>, in Bruce Hahne's 2010 BVH conversion (cgspeed.com), fetched from the mirror <https://github.com/una-dinosauria/cmu-mocap> | Free for any use, see below |
| `vendor/GLTFLoader.js` | three.js r160 glTF loader, unmodified | <https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r160/examples/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js> | MIT (`vendor/three.LICENSE`) |
| `vendor/BufferGeometryUtils.js` | three.js r160, unmodified (the loader imports it) | <https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r160/examples/jsm/utils/BufferGeometryUtils.js> | MIT (`vendor/three.LICENSE`) |
| `vendor/SkeletonUtils.js` | three.js r160, unmodified (clones the rigged model per player) | <https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r160/examples/jsm/utils/SkeletonUtils.js> | MIT (`vendor/three.LICENSE`) |

## MakeHuman

MakeHuman's licence (LICENSE.md in its repository) releases the bundled assets, "any data
contributing to the graphical output", under CC0 1.0 Universal, and says of exports: "As
the assets have been released under CC0, there is no limitation on what you can do with"
them. The files used carry the notice "(c) 2020 Data Collection AB, Joel Palmius, Jonas
Hauquier" and "license: CC0". No attribution is required; it is given here anyway.

The MakeHuman files themselves are not in this repository. The build script downloads them
into `.mocap-cache/makehuman/` (gitignored). The body is simplified with pyfqmr (MIT), a
build-time dependency only.

## CMU motion capture

From mocap.cs.cmu.edu: "This data is free for use in research and commercial projects
worldwide. If you publish results obtained using this data, we would appreciate it if you
would send the citation to your published paper to jkh+mocap@cs.cmu.edu, and also would add
this text to your acknowledgments section: The data used in this project was obtained from
mocap.cs.cmu.edu. The database was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217."

Bruce Hahne's conversion notes (READMEFIRST.txt in the mirror): "CMU places no restrictions
on the use of the original dataset, and I (Bruce) place no additional restrictions on the
use of this particular BVH conversion." CMU's FAQ adds that the data may be included in
commercial products but not resold as motion data.

The BVH files themselves are not in this repository. The build script downloads the trials
below into `.mocap-cache/` (gitignored).

Not motion-captured as such (no free clip was found): the dunk, the hook shot, the screen
and the block are the nearest captured motion adapted as noted above. The viewer also lifts
the collarbone with a raised arm, which the mocap does not record, and re-poses a blocker's
or contester's arm so the hand goes to the ball, a dunker's so it goes to the rim, and both arms of a
hook shot (our own code, no further source). A left-handed player's shots and dunk are the same
clips mirrored by the viewer.

## Not included

No Mixamo (Adobe) characters or animations are used, in the repository or at run time. No
real player's likeness is used: every player is the same generic body, varied by height,
weight, wingspan, skin tone, hair and gear.

## Rebuilding

    python tools/build_player_assets.py

needs numpy, pyfqmr and network access to GitHub for the first run. The clip list, the cut
points and the timing marks (`extras.hoopsim` in the glTF) are at the top of the script.
